How a CPU Actually Works

Below is the wiring diagram of a working RISC-V processor. Press play and watch a real program run through it: the bytes come out of memory, get decoded, get executed, and the answer travels back into a register. Every number that moves is a real value from a real run.

Press play. The clock will tick forward one step at a time.
Each tick is broken into its separate moments so you can read them in order.
1

Five steps, always the same

Fetch the instruction from memory. Decode it. Execute it. Touch memory if it needs to. Save the answer. That is all a CPU ever does.

2

Five instructions at once

Watch the cards along the top of the diagram. While one is being executed, the next is being decoded and another is being fetched. That overlap is the pipeline.

3

Gaps are real

When a step has nothing to do you will see an empty slot, and the caption will say so. Those gaps are called bubbles, and getting rid of them is most of what CPU design is about.
